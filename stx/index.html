Seagate Technology Holdings plc · Ordinary shares · Singapore / Dublin

AI needs somewhere to keep its data, and Seagate is sold out.

Hard drives still hold most of the world's cloud data, and AI has made them scarce. Seagate's revenue rose 48% last quarter to $3.6B at a record 52% gross margin, its new HAMR drives are ramping, and nearline capacity is largely allocated through 2027. The worry: rivals like Toshiba are now adding capacity.

Watch list

What moves the thesis, sorted by direction.

Tailwind

Sold out into 2027

Nearline drive capacity is reported to be almost fully allocated through calendar 2027, and long-term agreements now stretch into 2028, which supports pricing.

Tailwind

HAMR margin lift

Mozaic HAMR drives store more per platter. The CFO said incremental gross margin is well above 60%, and half of HAMR exabytes should be on Mozaic 4 by the end of 2026.

Tailwind

Cash for shareholders

Free cash flow reached $1.1B last quarter and $3.1B for FY26. Seagate repaid $1.4B of debt in the year and pays a dividend while buying back stock.

Risk

Toshiba adds capacity

On Oct 2 a report that Toshiba will double its AI hard-drive capacity by fiscal 2027 knocked 10% off Seagate. More supply could end the shortage pricing sooner.

Watch

TDK heads unit

Bloomberg reported on Oct 6 that Seagate and Toshiba are bidding for TDK's magnetic-heads business, worth up to several billion dollars. The stock fell 9% that day.

Risk

A cyclical industry

Drive makers have swung from shortage to glut before: Seagate lost money in several quarters of FY23–24. A slowdown in cloud spending would hit pricing fast.
