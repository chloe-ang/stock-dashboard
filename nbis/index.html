Nebius Group N.V. · Class A shares · Amsterdam

Renting out AI supercomputers, and building them as fast as it can.

Nebius runs its own GPU data centers and rents the computing power to AI labs and companies. Revenue grew 454% last quarter to $582M, run-rate revenue hit $3B, and management targets $7–9B by December. To get there it plans $20–25B of capex this year, paid for with debt, convertible bonds and customer prepayments.

Watch list

What moves the thesis, sorted by direction.

Tailwind

Demand outruns supply

Run-rate revenue rose 58% in one quarter to $3B, and on-demand GPU prices were raised 17–21% on Oct 1. Customers are signing multi-year contracts with large prepayments.

Tailwind

Prepayments fund the build

Most new deals came with prepayments covering 50–60% of the related capex; management expects more than $9B of prepayments in 2026.

Watch

The second-half ramp

H1 revenue was $981M, so the $3.0–3.4B guide needs $2.0–2.4B in H2, roughly double the June run rate. Q3 shows whether new capacity is landing on time.

Risk

Heavy spending and debt

Capex was $5.7B in Q2 against $582M of revenue, and debt reached $10.2B. Analysts expect Nebius to raise more debt or equity, which could dilute shareholders.

Risk

GPU pricing and competition

Nebius competes with CoreWeave and the big clouds. If GPU supply loosens, rental prices could fall before the new data centers pay back.

Watch

Software and inference

The Token Factory inference service and the Oct 1 purchase of Inferize aim to lift margins beyond raw GPU rental.
